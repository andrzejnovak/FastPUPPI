I · The problem · Trigger

Two trigger levels. Level-1 (L1) is hardware: FPGAs that look at every bunch crossing (40 MHz) and decide within 12.5 microseconds which 750 thousand per second to keep. It sees calorimeter and muon data and, new for Phase-2, charged-particle tracks: the outer tracker has paired-sensor modules that let FPGAs find tracks above 2 GeV for every crossing. The pixel detector (the inner tracker, closest to the beam) is not part of that: its chips hold hits until the L1 accept and only then send them out. After an accept, everything is read out to the high-level trigger (HLT), a software farm that stores about 7.5 kHz. 40 MHz L1 scouting is a separate system that processes the L1 objects of every crossing, also the rejected ones. ATLAS comparison: its Phase-2 L0 runs at 1 MHz within 10 microseconds, with no inner-tracker tracking in the decision.
