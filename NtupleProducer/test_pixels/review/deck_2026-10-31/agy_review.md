## (a) The 5 Biggest Problems with the Flow

1. **Slide 4 spoils the hardware roadblock of Slide 11, leaving the 5-slide arc of Idea A (Slides 6–10) unmotivated.**
   - [Slide 4 (`trigger`)](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/project/slides/trigger.html) is titled *"The CMS Phase-2 trigger: pixels arrive too late"* and states that pixel hits wait in the chips until an L1 accept. Then Slides 6–10 develop an L1 pixel-tracking algorithm as if pixel hits *are* available, only for [Slide 11 (`chip`)](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/project/slides/chip.html) to present *"The catch: the pixel chip has no early read"* as a reveal. Worse, Slides 6–10 never state the hypothesis Idea A was actually testing: **an early regional readout request of ~70 pixel chips around L1 jets within 1–5 µs** (which is buried in [Backup Slide 18 (`bandwidth`)](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/project/slides/bandwidth.html)).
   - **Fix**: Make Slide 4 about the baseline trigger architecture (*"The CMS Phase-2 trigger: L1 selects 750 kHz from 40 MHz in 12.5 µs using outer-tracker tracks"*). Open Idea A (Slide 6/7) by stating the regional-readout hypothesis explicitly (*"If L1 could pull pixel hits in narrow windows around jets, would pixel b-tagging rescue low-mass HH→4b?"*). After showing that the physics works (Slides 6–9), deliver the hardware blocker immediately on Slide 10 (`chip`), followed by `robust` (Slide 11) reframed around what a *future* fast-readout pixel upgrade would require ($\ge 2$ layers, priority queues, 64 bits/jet).

2. **Two different "60%" baselines and four different efficiency denominators are mixed across slides without warning.**
   - By coincidence, the planned 19 kHz $H_T$ seeds keep **60%** of *all* $HH\to 4b$ events (all $m_{HH}$, no offline cuts) AND **60%** of *offline-usable low-mass* ($m_{HH} < 400\text{ GeV}$) events, while keeping **36%** of *all low-mass* ($m_{HH} < 400\text{ GeV}$) events:

Doing the same thing on [Slide 16 (`summary`)](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/project/slides/summary.html) (Bullets 1 and 4) leaves the audience confused at both ends of the talk.
     - [Slide 9 (`seed`)](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/project/slides/seed.html) quotes the +2.5 kHz **offline-usable low-mass** numbers (`today's NN 83% · pixel 96%`), and then 10 seconds later [Slide 10 (`robust`)](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/project/slides/robust.html) quotes the +2.5 kHz **all-event** numbers (`today's NN 69.5% · pixel 77.8%`) under the unlabelled header `HH kept, +2.5 kHz seed`.

## (d) What Is Confusing or Likely to Be Misread in the Figures & Diagrams

1. **[figs/gap_mhh.png](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/figs/gap_mhh.png) (Slide 5)**:
   - **Red labels on bars are % *missed*, while slide text quotes % *kept***: The red numbers printed above every bar (`77%`, `74%`, `59%`, `43%` on the left; `60%`, `32%`, `20%` on the right) are the **missed** fraction per bin, whereas the slide bullets quote the **kept** fraction (`60%`, `36%`, `60%`). Notably, the `300–350 GeV` bin on the right panel is labelled `60%` (meaning 60% *missed*), right next to the slide bullet saying `60% kept below 400 GeV`!
   - **Missing red percentage label on the lowest bin (`250–300 GeV`) of the right panel**: The first bin on the right panel has a small red bar (~5 events, nearly 100% missed) with no percentage printed above it.
   - **Identical top panels (`κλ info [%]`)**: The top-left and top-right histograms are 100% identical (both show the generator-level signal-only Fisher information *before* any trigger or offline cuts). Placing the pre-selection generator curve directly above `Analysis level: events an offline 4b + 3b selection would keep` misleads viewers into thinking the top-right curve shows the information distribution *after* offline selection (where the $250\text{--}350\text{ GeV}$ bins are heavily suppressed by the $p_T > 30\text{ GeV}$ jet cut).
   - **Wide last bin (`800–1000 GeV`)**: The last bin spans 200 GeV (4× wider than the 50 GeV bins) and includes overflow ($m_{HH} > 1\text{ TeV}$), while the y-axis says `events in 3000 fb⁻¹ per 50 GeV`.

2. **[figs/d0_resolution.png](file:///work/submit/novaka/l1pixels/review/deck_2026-10-31/figs/d0_resolution.png) (Slide 6)**:
   - **Shared-hit residual vs. true resolution**: The x-axis is `d0 - d0(HLT pixel track) [µm]`. Because the pixel-extended L1 track and the HLT pixel track use the **same pixel clusters**, the $35\ \mu\text{m}$ width is the residual between two fits sharing hits, not the true $d_0$ resolution relative to the generated particle ($d_0 - d_0^{\text{true}}$). A postdoc or PI will spot this immediately; either plot $d_0 - d_0^{\text{true}}$ or explicitly label the legend `residual vs. HLT pixel track: 35 µm`.
   - **Unlabelled overflow spikes at $\pm 1500\ \mu\text{m}$**: The tall spikes in the outermost bins (`-1500` and `+1500 µm`) are underflow/overflow bins (for the orange curve, wrong pixel-hit combinations in the $\pm 1.5\text{ mm}$ window), but have no `< -1500` / `> +1500` labels.
