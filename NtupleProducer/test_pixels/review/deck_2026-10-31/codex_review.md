**(a) The five biggest flow problems**

1. **The denominator changes without enough warning.**  
   “36% kept,” “60% kept,” and “93% kept” concern different populations. Moreover, the HH study uses two HT seeds, while several labels say “planned menu.” Reserve that term for the full menu. Introduce three explicit populations on slide 5: **all HH events → low-mass HH events → low-mass events passing the mock offline selection**. Use the same names thereafter.

2. **The opening gives results before establishing their meaning.**  
   Slide 2 introduces self-coupling information, b-tag efficiency, mistag, scouting, significance and added rate before explaining them. Replace its numerical executive summary with a three-branch roadmap: **improve the L1 decision / analyse rejected crossings / optimise the existing trigger inputs**. Define terms when first used; the backup glossary cannot teach them retrospectively.

3. **The transition from a better track to more accepted events is missing.**  
   Slides 6–8 explain tracks and per-jet tagging; slide 9 suddenly introduces an event trigger optimised at fixed background rate. Add a small diagram: **four jets → four b-scores → summed score plus HT requirement → one accept/reject decision**. Explain that “+2.5 kHz” counts additional background crossings that the HT seeds reject.

4. **The strength of several conclusions exceeds the displayed evidence.**  
   The 35 µm number is a residual against a correlated reference; production-chip behaviour still needs confirmation; the scouting result concerns one analysis with sparse genuine-4b simulation; and the NN seed still needs deployment studies. These qualifications belong beside the relevant conclusions, not only in notes or backup. Replace “what works” with **“the practical candidate to validate.”**

5. **The central argument loses momentum twice.**  
   The robustness table interrupts the transition from pixel benefit to readout feasibility. The other-channel survey interrupts the final HH recommendation. Move slides 10 and 15 to backup. Use the saved speaking time to explain the figures and the practical comparison on slide 14.
